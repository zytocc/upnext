# Backlog verdicts

A backlog verdict is a short public answer about how a game fits a backlog and limited time: how far you got, whether it was worth it, how long until it clicked, and what made you put it down. Every install that turns them on reads everyone's answers on game pages, pick cards and the Library's filters.

## Writing one

A game's page has **Write a backlog verdict**. After you mark a game finished, shelved, abandoned, burnt out or not for me it offers one in a line (Settings › Library data turns the offer off). UpNext fills in what it already knows from your mark, playtime and the reasons you gave for stopping. You change or add the rest, and a short text is optional.

Before your first verdict leaves the computer, a consent screen says it will be public under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). Nothing is sent until you press Send.

A verdict goes with your player's random key, made on your first send, and never your name, a store account or your library. Hours go as a range. [A verdict](verdicts/verdict.md) has the full list.

## Taking it down

**Take down** on the game page withdraws a verdict. **Take down all my verdicts** in Settings › Library data withdraws them all and forgets your key. A withdrawn verdict is gone from the service within a week, but copies people already downloaded can't be recalled.

## Reading other players' verdicts

**Backlog verdicts from other players**, in Settings › Library data and in the setup wizard, downloads everyone's verdicts once a day. The last download stays for offline use. A second switch there decides whether the AI that picks reads them too.

## The service

UpNext's service is at `https://verdicts.upnext.games`, run by UpNext contributors. Its terms are at [`/terms`](https://verdicts.upnext.games/terms) and on the consent screen. To use another one, set its address in Settings › Library data or `verdicts_url`. A computer that can't reach it can use a downloaded snapshot with `upnext verdicts apply`. [The contract](VERDICTS.md) describes the endpoint and how to run your own.
